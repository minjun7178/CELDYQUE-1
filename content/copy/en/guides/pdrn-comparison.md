# Comparing CELDYQUE's three PDRN products

The CELDYQUE PDRN collection includes a liquid concentrate, a face serum and a cream. Start with the product format and the step you want in your routine. The percentage in a product name does not, by itself, tell you which formula is the better choice.

This guide compares CELDYQUE products. It is not an independent ranking of the wider PDRN market.

## Start with the step you need

For a **cream step**, look at the PDRN 12% + EGF Peptide Cream. Its role in the existing directions is different from the two liquids: it is applied as the moisturizer.

For a **separate serum step**, compare the PDRN 12% + EGF Peptide Serum with the PDRN concentrate. Both appear before moisturizer in the current directions, but the product descriptions and ingredient summaries differ. Check the exact application method, full ingredients and label instructions before deciding.

For a **mix-in method**, the concentrate's existing page describes combining it with a compatible serum or cream. That does not establish that every product combination is appropriate. Use only the method supported by the directions for your product.

You do not need to use all three simply because they belong to the same collection.

## What do 25%, 12% and 250,000 ppm mean?

These figures appear in the current product names and descriptions. The serum page also uses the phrase “PDRN complex.” A supplied ingredient complex and its individual components are not interchangeable descriptions.

A meaningful concentration comparison requires the same substance, measurement basis and formulation context. Until those definitions are clear for each formula, do not read the numbers as a ranking of effectiveness, suitability or expected results.

## Compare more than one highlighted ingredient

The concentrate's current summary lists Sodium DNA / PDRN, EGF and panthenol. The serum highlights PDRN-related ingredients, EGF, peptides and Volufiline alongside moisturizing ingredients. The cream lists PDRN-related ingredients, EGF and Volufiline together with ingredients such as ceramide, panthenol and squalane.

These are selected summaries, not complete ingredient declarations. The full list matters when you need to identify a particular ingredient or compare two formulas accurately.

## Questions before choosing

### Does the higher number mean a stronger product?

Not on the basis of the information alone. Confirm what the figure describes and compare the whole formula and directions. A product-name percentage is not a finished-product performance test.

### Can the serum and cream be used in the same routine?

The current directions place serum before cream. Follow both labels and introduce unfamiliar products carefully rather than changing several steps at once.

### Are these products intended for procedures or damaged skin?

They are presented as topical cosmetics. Do not infer suitability for broken skin or immediate post-procedure use from the ingredient name. Ask the treating clinician when to resume cosmetics after a procedure.
